Riparian vegetation along the Rio Grande Corridor over time

By Francisco Logan, Katie Slack, and Emily Smith

    About this project

    This web map illustrates the abundance and distribution of riparian vegetation along the Rio Grande Corridor of New Mexico. This app supplements the vegetation distribution with two climate factors that could potentially impact riparian ecotones, precipitation, and carbon dioxide emissions. This app seeks to show the variance in riparian vegetation over time, by displaying distribution maps for the years 2000 - 2015 at a 3 year interval. Vegetation and precipitation are represented spatially along the interactive map. Carbon Emissions are displayed as single average per year for the state of New Mexico.

    This map is made for anyone interested in New Mexico ecozones, vegetation change over time and environmental change during the current period of global warming. The developers seek to illustrate the effects of human-caused climate change on New Mexico’s riparian regions, by focusing on the Rio Grande Corridor over the past 20 years. This map can be used by legislators, educators and anyone interested in environmental change. Please see our citations to the right to see where the data was obtained and learn more about riparian area conservation in New Mexico.
